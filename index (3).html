Research

The Calibration Law: diagnosing and repairing answer-position bias in Mixture-of-Experts vision–language models

Independent project, Jan 2026 – present. Manuscript in preparation for ICLR 2027. With two collaborators; I lead the project.

MoE vision–language models fail badly at telling apart images that differ in one small detail. On VisMin, five open models (DeepSeek-VL2, MolmoE, ERNIE-4.5-VL; 1B–28B) answer "First" on 94–100% of paired questions regardless of content. We show this answer-position bias is the failure, introduce a zero-cost diagnostic for it (the bias index), and find that training-free interventions — router steering, prompting, logit calibration — succeed exactly in proportion to how close they bring the bias index to 50%.

A label-free recipe triples the VisMin group score (9.0 → 25.8), transfers zero-shot to Winoground and ColorSwap, and is carried causally by a single final-layer prefill routing decision. Along the way we found and corrected a scoring artifact in the official VisMin evaluator worth up to +33 group points.

When can a second pass fix document structure? An end-to-end anatomy of hierarchy repair

Laputa, 2026. Manuscript in preparation for ICDAR 2027. I proposed and lead the project.

PDF extractors recover text well and document hierarchy poorly. In a pre-registered 18-run study we asked whether a cheap second pass can repair the hierarchy: a self-supervised 115M pointer model trained on pseudo-labels mined from section numbering and embedded PDF outlines, with no human annotation, evaluated end-to-end against deterministic rules, a 26B LLM, and the supervised 4B multimodal state of the art on three benchmarks.

Deep, unnumbered book hierarchy turns out to be invisible to text-side and crop-level methods alike. But roughly 95% of those books ship outline metadata every published method ignores — and a zero-label, CPU-only anchoring cascade matches the 4B model in the hardest regime at about 1/500th of its inference cost.
